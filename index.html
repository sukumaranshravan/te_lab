1. Flakiness & Elongation Index (Shape Test)

Ref: IS 2386 (Part 1) – 1963, Cl. 4 & 5; combined index limit as per MoRTH. Applies to aggregate coarser than 6.3 mm.

Apparatus

  • Thickness gauge and length gauge
  • IS sieves 63, 50, 40, 31.5, 25, 20, 16, 12.5, 10 and 6.3 mm
  • Balance (5 kg capacity, readable to 1 g)

Procedure

  • Sieve the sample through the set of IS sieves and separate it into the size fractions.
  • From each fraction take at least 200 pieces and weigh it (W). Fractions with too little material can be ignored.
  • Flakiness: pass each piece of a fraction through the matching slot of the thickness gauge (slot = 0.6 × mean size of the fraction).
  • Collect all pieces that pass the slot (flaky particles) and weigh them (w).
  • Weigh the pieces retained on the thickness gauge (non-flaky) – this is W − w.
  • Elongation: try each non-flaky piece in the matching opening of the length gauge (opening = 1.8 × mean size). Pieces that cannot pass through with their long side are elongated.
  • Weigh the elongated pieces retained on the length gauge (x) for each fraction.
  • Enter W, w and x for each fraction on the first tab.

Calculation & limits

  • Flakiness Index = (Σw ÷ ΣW) × 100
  • Elongation Index = (Σx ÷ Σ(W − w)) × 100
  • Combined index = Flakiness + Elongation. A commonly used limit for road aggregate is 30% (MoRTH); some specs set other limits, so check your project specification.

2. Angularity Number

Ref: IS 2386 (Part 1) – 1963, Cl. 6.

Apparatus

  • Metal cylinder, closed at one end, about 3 litres (diameter ≈ height)
  • Tamping rod, 16 mm diameter, 60 cm long, rounded at one end
  • Metal scoop (~1 litre heaped), balance (10 kg, readable to 1 g), IS sieves

Procedure

  • Calibrate the cylinder: weigh it empty, fill it with water, and weigh again. Water weight = C.
  • Take single-size aggregate retained between a pair of sieves (20–16, 16–12.5, 12.5–10, 10–6.3 or 6.3–4.75 mm). Wash and oven-dry it, then cool.
  • Fill the cylinder in three layers. Compact each layer with 100 blows of the rod (about 2 blows per second, rod lifted about 5 cm and dropped), spread evenly over the surface.
  • After the third layer, fill to overflowing and strike off the surplus using the rod as a straight edge.
  • Weigh the cylinder with aggregate. Repeat for three separate fillings and take the mean weight of aggregate (W).
  • Determine the specific gravity of the aggregate (G).
  • In this page, enter the cylinder weight, the cylinder + aggregate weight (use the mean of the three), the cylinder + water weight and G.

Calculation & interpretation

  • Angularity Number = 67 − (100 W ÷ C G), reported to the nearest whole number.
  • Values range roughly from 0 (rounded) to about 11 (very angular). Around 7–10 is generally preferred for road work.
  • Not suitable for aggregate that breaks down during compaction.

3. Aggregate Impact Value

Ref: IS 2386 (Part 4) – 1963; limits from IS 383.

Apparatus

  • Impact testing machine (steel cup 102 mm dia, hammer of about 14 kg with 380 mm free fall)
  • Cylindrical measure, 75 mm dia × 50 mm deep
  • Tamping rod, 10 mm dia, 230 mm long, rounded at one end
  • IS sieves 12.5, 10 and 2.36 mm; balance readable to 0.1 g; oven (100–110 °C)

Procedure

  • Sieve the aggregate; the test sample is the portion passing 12.5 mm and retained on 10 mm.
  • Dry the sample in an oven at 100–110 °C for 4 hours and cool to room temperature.
  • Fill the measure in three layers (about one-third depth each), giving each layer 25 gentle blows with the tamping rod. Strike off the surplus.
  • Weigh the aggregate in the measure (W1).
  • Transfer the whole sample to the cup, fixed firmly on the machine base, and tamp it 25 times.
  • Raise the hammer until its lower face is 380 mm above the aggregate surface and let it fall freely. Give a total of 15 blows, at least 1 second apart.
  • Remove the crushed aggregate and sieve it through the 2.36 mm sieve until almost nothing more passes.
  • Weigh the fraction passing 2.36 mm (W2) and the fraction retained (W3). W2 + W3 should be within 1 g of W1, otherwise repeat the test.
  • Do two trials and take the mean.

Calculation & limits

  • Impact Value (%) = (W2 ÷ W1) × 100, reported to the nearest whole number as the mean of the two trials.
  • Common grading: below 10% exceptionally strong; 10–20% strong; 20–30% satisfactory for road surfacing; above 35% weak for road surfacing.
  • IS 383: max 30% for wearing surfaces, 45% for other concrete.
